Squirrel Studies

Five takes on one character, each drawn in the same five poses. Every shape is a smooth spline: there are no straight lines, only curves that meet in soft points at the nose, toes, elbows, knees and fur tips. The figure is a flat dark-grey silhouette; thin white lines show where limbs, head and tail overlap, and the white of the eyes carries the whole expression. Run cycle · v2

One full gallop stride in eight key poses, studied frame by frame from slow-motion footage of a gray squirrel running flat out (used privately as reference; not reproduced here). The loops interpolate smoothly between the keys. Notice how level the head stays, how the rump rises above the shoulders as the forepaws land, and how far the hind feet swing past them before landing. A real squirrel does about five of these strides a second.

What v2 took from the photographs

    • Proportions: a rounder, higher-crowned head with a shorter, blunter snout and the eye set mid-head; a deep chest and slimmer waist; a heavy haunch and a long hind foot that dominate the rear silhouette; a tail about as long as head and body together, narrow at the root and widest two-thirds of the way out.
